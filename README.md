> **Exploring the mathematical foundations of noisy data modeling, probabilistic inference, and signal decomposition — with applications to quantitative finance.**

This repository collects implementations focused on the statistical fundamentals underlying **Quantitative Analysis** and **Signal Processing**. The goal is not to apply libraries out of the box, but to investigate the mathematical behavior of models under uncertainty — understanding *why* they work before *how* to use them.

---

## 🔬 Modules

### 1. Polynomial Regression & Overfitting
Studying the impact of model complexity (polynomial degree) on generalization error, with a focus on the **Bias-Variance Tradeoff**. Directly applicable to backtesting in finance — where overfitting to historical data is one of the most common sources of strategy failure.

### 2. Bayesian Hypothesis Selection
Applying Bayes' Theorem to select the best hypothesis given a noisy dataset. The algorithm computes the posterior probability $P(h|D)$ for each hypothesis, updating beliefs incrementally as new data arrives — without full retraining. Relevant to **Regime Switching** models and real-time probability updating in trading systems.

### 3. Stochastic Signal Analysis
Analyzing random signals with a focus on statistical properties: mean, variance, and autocorrelation. Analogous to the study of asset return distributions, volatility modeling, and noise filtering in financial time series.
